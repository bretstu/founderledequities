About.

What this is. Founder Led Equities shows what the chief executive of every US public company worth $1 billion or more owns of the company they run: the share, in percent and in dollars, as of their newest filing, and how that share has moved through every purchase and sale since 2016. About 2,100 companies are covered. Roughly 300 of them are run by a founder, meaning the company's own proxy statement says so, and those are the ones the site is about.

What counts as ownership. Shares held, not shares obtainable: every class of stock the filing lists, counted one for one, over the shares outstanding on the company’s cover page. Options, unvested awards and other rights to acquire shares are not counted, so the site’s figure is lower than a proxy statement’s, which adds shares a person could acquire within sixty days. A company whose chief executive holds the business through partnership units exchangeable into the stock (an “Up-C” or a REIT’s operating partnership) is left out of the site unless the filings prove the paired voting shares count the units one for one; the number the site cannot compute honestly it does not show.

What counts as a move. One rule, used by the activity page’s “Moved the stake” chip, the weekly letter, and the alerts on a company page: an open-market purchase or a discretionary sale of any size, or any other filing (a planned sale, a grant, a gift, an exercise) that changes the holding by 1% or more, measured against the holding itself, not the company. A share count restated on a cover page is not a move.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure is computed from the forms insiders sign with the SEC (Forms 3, 4 and 5), read from EDGAR by a pipeline that runs every night, and for founders within minutes of a filing during the day. Nothing is estimated and nothing is copied from a third party. Every number on a company page links to the filing it came from.

What ownership means here

Economic ownership: the shares the person beneficially owns, directly and through trusts, family and holding companies, across every class of stock, divided by the company's shares outstanding from its latest 10-Q or 10-K cover page. Options, units and awards that have not vested are not counted, because they are not shares yet. When a filing leaves a gap the record cannot reconcile, the page says so rather than guessing.

What a trade means here

Every purchase and sale is classified by what the person actually decided:

  • Boughtshares bought on the open market, with their own money.
  • Discretionaryshares sold at a time the person chose.
  • Plannedshares sold under a plan set months earlier (Rule 10b5-1); the decision was made long before the sale.
  • Compensationoptions exercised and sold, or shares withheld for tax; the stake did not move the way a purchase or sale moves it.

The distinction is the point. A founder who buys, or who sells outside a plan, has decided something this week. A planned sale was decided last year.

Why

The person running a company is the one insider whose stake is a statement about it. Whether they still own it, and what they did about it this week, is public in the filings but hard to read there. This site reads them.

Every company, every stake, every trade, every alert and every export on this site is free. Nothing here is investment advice. If a number here is wrong, write to corrections@founderledequities.com with the filing, and it will be read.
