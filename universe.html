The rules

Who is in. Every company in the SEC's own registry of tickered registrants that files a 10-K (a domestic filer), has at least one Form 3, 4 or 5 on record, and had a market capitalization at or above $1 billion on the snapshot date. One row per company: a dual-class filer counts once.

Who is out, and why. Foreign private issuers file 20-Fs and their officers file no ownership forms; Section 16 does not reach them, so this site cannot measure them. Blank-check companies (SIC 6770) have no chief executive in the sense this site means. Companies the sizing rules could not place above the bar.

How size is decided. Two independent measures: the market cap published by the price vendor, and the company's own cover-page share count (from its most recently filed report) times the newest close. When they agree within 25%, the company is sized. When they disagree, it is admitted if either clears the bar and flagged for a human reading. When neither is available, a current member is kept and a newcomer waits: a counting failure never evicts and never admits.

The edge does not flap. A company enters at $1 billion and leaves only after two consecutive quarterly snapshots below $800 million. Otherwise names near the line would blink in and out, their histories appearing and vanishing.

Snapshots are quarterly and dated. This list is what the rules produced on the date shown, from the sources named. A company that crossed $1 billion after that date joins at the next snapshot. If you believe a company is missing in error, write to corrections@founderledequities.com with the ticker; the evidence behind every decision is kept.

Sources: SEC EDGAR (company registry, filing histories, cover-page share counts); Polygon (market capitalization and closing prices). The rules are code, in fle/market_universe.py, and this page is regenerated from that code's evidence file at each snapshot.
